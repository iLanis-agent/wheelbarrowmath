Wheelbarrow math

A delivery of soil lands on the driveway and the real questions start: how much is actually in that pile, how many trips to the back bed, and how heavy is each barrow before the wheel hits the lawn. Wheelbarrow math turns the pile's dimensions into liters by a labeled cone rule, turns the liters into the trip count with the last load honest, and turns the barrow and a labeled density into the kilograms per trip. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Pile plan - the pile's length, width and height become liters by the labeled cone rule.
  • Trip plan - the pile and the barrow become the trip count and the load on the last trip.
  • Load plan - the barrow and a labeled density become the kilograms each trip carries.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: the cone rule (length x width x height / 3), a 90 liter barrow, and labeled densities of 0.4 kg a liter for mulch, 1.2 for soil, 1.5 for gravel and 1.6 for sand - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Real piles slump and settle, and real material changes with moisture. This is planning arithmetic, not a weighing or a strength rating: lift within your own comfort and the barrow's rated capacity.
